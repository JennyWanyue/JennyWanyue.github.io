Ph.D. Student / Stochastic Control & Formal Methods

I am a Ph.D. student in Electrical Engineering and Computer Sciences at the UC Berkeley.

I study stochastic control, formal verification, and optimization for cyber-physical systems, with a focus on probabilistic guarantees for autonomous systems under uncertainty.

Before Berkeley, I earned a B.S. in Systems Science and Engineering at Washington University in St. Louis, with a second major in Physics and minors in Electrical Engineering and Music. At WashU, I worked on networked control, quantum materials, and machine learning for scientific discovery.

03CURRENT WORK

Research

I work at the intersection of control theory, formal methods, and data-driven uncertainty quantification, with a particular interest in mathematical guarantees for safety-critical systems.

01 / BERKELEY Ongoing

Control Synthesis under Stochastic Temporal Logic

Developing optimization-based control synthesis for discrete-time stochastic systems subject to polyhedral stochastic signal temporal logic (StSTL) specifications. The goal is to synthesize control policies with meaningful probabilistic satisfaction guarantees.

Stochastic ControlStSTLOptimizationFormal Methods
02 / BERKELEYManuscript under review

Data-Driven Verification & Parameter Synthesis

Studying distribution-free verification and parameter synthesis for temporal-logic specifications using finite stochastic trajectory samples. The approach combines Clopper–Pearson confidence intervals, scenario optimization, and compositional robustness analysis.

PAC GuaranteesUncertainty QuantificationScenario Optimization
03 / WASHUACC 2025

Wireless Networked Control for UAV Formations

Optimized communication and control under channel fading and packet loss using Lyapunov-based stability analysis, Bernstein-type probability bounds, and semidefinite programming.
